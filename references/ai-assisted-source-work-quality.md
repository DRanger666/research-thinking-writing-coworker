# AI-Assisted Source-Work Quality

## Load when

Load this file when the user asks for AI-assisted scientific writing, literature-review assistance boundaries, source summarization, source comparison, citation/reference checking, AI-polished prose audit, manuscript/proposal defensibility after AI use, disclosure/compliance awareness, or a judgment about whether an AI-assisted workflow is safe enough for serious scientific work.

Do not load this file for ordinary non-AI drafting unless the task involves source visibility, citation reliability, authorial ownership, or submission-facing defensibility.

## Runtime role

P027 is a cross-cutting quality-control gate for AI-assisted academic writing and source work. It translates Cheng, Calhoun, and Reedy (2025) into this skill's operational language:

- preserve the researcher's intellectual ownership;
- keep evidence traceable to visible sources, data, notes, or user-authored draft material;
- verify citations and source support;
- prevent source-invisible literature claims and fabricated references;
- preserve meaning, voice, nuance, and claim scope during AI-assisted revision;
- keep journal, funder, committee, supervisor, institutional, and publisher rules as current-source questions when they matter.

P027 is not a moral doctrine for the whole skill, not a seventh cluster, not a ban on source-grounded AI-assisted literature work, not a ban on AI-assisted ideation or hypothesis pressure-testing, and not current journal-policy authority.

## Core distinction

| Workflow type | Runtime interpretation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Source-grounded AI assistance | AI works on supplied papers, abstracts, notes, retrieved sources, data summaries, or user-authored drafts. | Proceed with source visibility, claim-evidence checks, and human verification. |
| AI-substituted source work | AI invents literature claims, generates references, interprets primary data, or certifies ethics/plagiarism/compliance without source control. | Stop and convert to a source-acquisition, verification, or human-owned analysis workflow. |

Use P016 as the positive literature-review workflow for interrogating visible papers and comparing evidence. Use P027 to quality-check that workflow. P027 should refine P016, not erase it.

## Risk tiers

| Use | Typical risk | Required control |
|---|---|---|
| Grammar, readability, translation, restructuring existing text | Lower risk | Check meaning, nuance, authorial voice, and claim strength. |
| Outlining from supplied material, summarizing visible sources, brainstorming from user-provided ideas, improving drafted content | Conditional | Verify source support, accuracy, originality, scope, and whether the output still reflects the user's analysis. |
| De novo section drafting from topic alone, research-idea or hypothesis generation treated as final user-owned contribution without critique/source grounding, primary data interpretation, literature review from model memory, reference generation, ethics/plagiarism certification | High risk | Do not treat output as submission-ready. Rebuild from visible sources, user-owned interpretation, and target-specific rules. |

## Quality-control checklist

Use this as an audit, not as a rigid ritual:

1. **Ownership:** Are the primary ideas, interpretations, and critical analyses still the researcher's?
2. **Source visibility:** Can every literature claim be traced to supplied, retrieved, or user-documented sources?
3. **Citation reliability:** Are references real, accurate, and actually supporting the attached claims?
4. **Claim scope:** Did AI make the prose broader, stronger, more causal, more current, or more general than the evidence allows?
5. **Meaning preservation:** Did AI polishing change technical meaning, hedge strength, theory, population, method, or result interpretation?
6. **Genre fit:** Does the AI-assisted output fit the target document: manuscript, proposal, thesis chapter, specific aims, committee memo, reviewer response, or outreach?
7. **Defensibility:** Could the user defend how the claim was produced under journal, reviewer, committee, funder, supervisor, or PI scrutiny?
8. **Disclosure/compliance:** If actual AI-use disclosure or policy compliance matters, use supplied guidance or retrieve current target rules before giving a compliance claim.

## Safe output forms

Choose the smallest useful artifact:

| User need | Produce |
|---|---|
| "Can I use AI for this?" | AI-use risk table plus safe workflow recommendation. |
| "Audit this AI-written section" | Claim-evidence-source audit, meaning-preservation notes, and rewrite priorities. |
| "Help me use AI on these papers" | Source-grounded P016 workflow plus P027 verification gates. |
| "Check citations/references" | Citation-reliability checklist and source-needed table; do not invent missing references. |
| "Do I need to disclose AI use?" | Target-rule retrieval plan or placeholder disclosure logic; do not claim current policy without sources. |
| "Make this AI-polished text submission-ready" | Meaning, claim-scope, evidence, and genre-fit audit before prose revision. |

## Handoffs

- Use `references/cluster-literature-review.md` when the task is source-grounded literature synthesis, theme grouping, related work, or Chapter 2/review architecture.
- Use `references/manuscript-and-results.md` when the issue affects whole-manuscript alignment, Results/preliminary-data communication, abstract alignment, or submission readiness.
- Use `references/peer-review-revision.md` when the issue is reviewer-facing: generic AI prose, citation mismatch, unsupported claims, weak synthesis, or revise-and-resubmit risk.
- Use `references/language-and-outreach.md` when the immediate task is polishing or academic phrasing, but keep P027 active if meaning, claim scope, or source support might change.
- Use `references/cluster-gap-problem.md` if AI brainstorming creates a candidate research idea, gap, RQ, aim, objective, or hypothesis that still needs source-grounded justification.
