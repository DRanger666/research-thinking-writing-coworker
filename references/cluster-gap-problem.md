# Gap and Problem Development

## Load when

Load this file after `SKILL.md` when the user is finding or testing a research idea, claiming novelty, asking what the gap is, drafting a literature gap statement or problem statement, shaping RQs/aims/objectives/hypotheses, or asking whether a study is worth doing. Load `references/routing-map.md` first when the task is vague or crosses proposal, literature review, manuscript, or reviewer-response layers.

Do not polish weak gap language before checking the underlying research logic. A candidate gap is not a fact until the user supplies or develops evidence from literature, data, practice reports, policy documents, prior-study limitations, or a credible field rationale.

## Core distinctions

| Item | Runtime meaning | Evidence needed | Typical next output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Candidate gap | A possible opportunity or inadequacy inferred from a topic, field pattern, population, method, theory, setting, variable, or practical problem. | Search trail, source notes, prior-study limitations, contradictions, excluded contexts, or practice evidence. | Gap taxonomy, search plan, originality map. |
| Literature gap statement | A compact literature-facing argument: what is known, where knowledge stops, why that missing piece matters, and how this study responds. | Citations or user-supplied summaries showing incompleteness, contradiction, limitation, or absence with stakes. | Gap paragraph, claim-evidence map. |
| Problem statement | A broader problem frame: big picture, specific issue, evidence, consequences, knowledge/practice gap, researchability, and lead-in to purpose/RQs. | Field evidence plus population/setting/context and consequences. | Problem-statement audit or rewrite. |
| Research question | A focused question that directly asks what the study will answer. | Gap plus feasible design boundary. | RQ alignment table. |
| Aim/objective | The study intention or measurable task that operationalizes the question. | Gap, design fit, variables/phenomenon, population/setting. | Aim/objective refinement. |
| Hypothesis | A testable expected relationship or difference, used only when the design and theory support prediction. | Theory, prior evidence, measurable variables, analytic route. | Hypothesis logic check. |

## From messy idea to usable research logic

1. **Name the starting material.** Capture topic, population/setting, phenomenon or variables, field, document genre, and intended audience. If missing, proceed with placeholders rather than inventing facts.
2. **Generate candidate routes, not conclusions.** Use P003 routes: explicit future-work statements, contradictions, methodological limits, excluded populations/settings, outdated evidence, theory limits, emerging variables/trends, unexplored relationships, prior recommendations, scale/scope limits, practice problems, and meta-view synthesis across studies.
3. **Classify the candidate gap.** Use P020 as a taxonomy, not proof: evidence, knowledge, practical, methodological, theoretical, empirical, and population gaps. Combining two dimensions can strengthen originality, but only if both are evidence-backed and coherent.
4. **Apply the P022 quality filter.** An untouched topic or exact variable combination is weak by itself. Stronger gaps show a meaningful inadequacy in existing research: contradictory findings, overused methods, missing populations, untested contexts, theory not explaining evidence, or practice needs not addressed by scholarship.
5. **Run the practical search loop.** Use P013 actions: search published literature, consult advisor/field expert when available, use digital tools and cited-paper trails, check influential journals, keep a query log, and research each question before turning it into a claim.
6. **Build the literature gap statement.** Use P017 sequence: context synthesis -> narrowed focus -> precise missing piece -> why it matters -> citations -> alignment with study aim -> transition to RQs/objectives.
7. **Build the problem statement.** Use P015 sequence: broad relevance -> specific issue -> evidence -> consequences -> knowledge/practice gap -> researchability -> focus justification -> field/theory link -> concise lead-in.
8. **Shape RQs/aims/objectives/hypotheses.** Each must answer the validated gap, fit the population/setting, and be feasible with the proposed method. Hypotheses require stronger predictive support than exploratory RQs.
9. **Package or hand off.** If the user needs a concept paper, proposal, Chapter 1, or supervisor memo, hand off to `references/cluster-front-end-proposal.md`. If the gap must be justified through a review architecture, hand off to `references/cluster-literature-review.md`.

## Gap routes and use cases

| Route | Use when | Audit question |
|---|---|---|
| Contradiction/tension | Prior findings disagree. | What missing variable, context, measure, theory, or design could explain the disagreement? |
| Methodological gap | Prior studies rely on one design, instrument, sample, or analytic frame. | Would a different method answer a question the dominant method cannot? |
| Population/context gap | Groups, regions, sectors, institutions, cultures, or settings are excluded. | Why should findings differ or matter in this population/context? |
| Theory gap | Existing theory does not explain a phenomenon or has not been tested in the context. | What theoretical assumption is incomplete, strained, or untested? |
| Empirical/evidence gap | A claim or theory lacks data in the target context. | What evidence is absent, and why is that absence consequential? |
| Practical gap | Practice, policy, or professional need outpaces research. | What happens in the real world if scholarship does not address it? |
| Temporal/emerging gap | New technology, behavior, crisis, policy, or social change alters the field. | Why might older evidence no longer fit current conditions? |
| Scale/scope gap | Studies are too localized, narrow, or limited for the user's aim. | Is expansion justified, or is it merely bigger? |

Preserve the v12 caveat on P020: the source has a likely inconsistency in the theoretical-gap row. Use the taxonomy operationally with caution and do not overclaim the source beyond its visible wording.

P013 search-loop guidance and P020 gap taxonomy are runtime scaffolds. They help organize research thinking, but they do not themselves prove field novelty, establish a complete search protocol, or verify that a gap exists.

## Gap-quality audit

Before drafting, check:

- **Absence-only novelty:** Does the claim merely say no one studied X plus Y? Strengthen with contradiction, excluded context, practice stakes, theory weakness, or method limitation.
- **Vague population/context:** Can the reader identify who, where, when, and under what conditions the issue matters?
- **Missing stakes:** What theoretical, practical, policy, methodological, or social consequence follows if the gap remains unaddressed?
- **Unsupported gap claim:** Which papers, notes, abstracts, reports, or search results support the claim? If none are supplied, label the gap as provisional.
- **Untestable or oversized aim:** Can the aim be answered with a manageable design, data source, sample, and analysis?
- **Gap/method mismatch:** Does the method actually address the gap type? A methodological gap may need a new design; a theory gap may need theory-testing or refinement; a practical gap may need implementation or stakeholder evidence.
- **Gap/problem confusion:** Is the user claiming a literature gap when they actually have a broad social problem, or claiming a problem without showing the literature/practice gap?
- **Reviewer visibility:** Following P024, can the opening two paragraphs show population/setting, the literature or response gap, and the stakes? If not, do not treat the problem statement as ready.

## Output selector

| User asks for | Produce | Include |
|---|---|---|
| "Find a gap" | Gap taxonomy | Candidate gaps, type, evidence needed, risk, next search. |
| "Make it original" | Originality map | Two-gap combinations, rationale, cautions, contribution wording. |
| "Is this a good problem statement?" | Problem-statement audit | Population/setting, specific issue, evidence, stakes, researchability, lead-in. |
| "Help with RQs/aims" | RQ/aim alignment table | Gap, RQ, aim/objective, hypothesis if warranted, method-fit note. |
| "How should I search?" | Search plan | Queries, databases/journals, inclusion boundaries, notes to track, decision criteria. |
| "Rewrite this" | Diagnostic rewrite | First state logic weaknesses; then provide a revised version with placeholders for missing evidence. |

## Handoffs

- Use `references/cluster-literature-review.md` when the gap must be supported by thematic synthesis, not just a gap paragraph.
- Use `references/cluster-front-end-proposal.md` after the gap/problem is validated and must become a concept paper, proposal, specific-aims-like page, Chapter 1, qual proposal, or supervisor memo.
- Use `references/manuscript-and-results.md` when the problem, aim, method, Results, and manuscript Introduction must align across a full paper.
- Use `references/method-fit-triage.md` when an RQ, aim, objective, hypothesis, evidence type, or broad design family must be checked for fit.
- Use `references/peer-review-revision.md` when the user wants a reviewer-facing risk audit or is responding to reviewer comments about novelty, gap, literature, or problem framing.
- Use `references/language-and-outreach.md` only after logic is sound or when the task is sentence-level reasoning refinement, hedging, significance wording, or research outreach.
