# Peer Review and Revision

## When to load

Load this file for pre-submission red-flag audits, reviewer-risk lists, revise-and-resubmit planning, major-revision strategy, reviewer comment diagnosis, response planning, claim-scope audits, and repair of manuscripts that reviewers may find weak.

Use it when the user asks what reviewers might dislike, why a manuscript may receive revisions, how to respond to major revisions, or how to convert reviewer comments into manuscript repairs. Do not offer generic reassurance when critique or repair is needed.

## Two modes

| Mode | Timing | Goal | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-submission red-flag audit | Before submission, resubmission, or advisor/committee review | Find problems likely to trigger criticism or major revision. | Reviewer-risk list, red-flag audit, claim-scope audit, repair plan. |
| Post-review revision response | After reviewer/editor comments arrive | Interpret comments, repair the manuscript, and answer every point with evidence and locations. | Revision matrix, response strategy, repair sequence, rebuttal logic when justified. |

Keep the modes distinct. A pre-submission audit is reviewer-facing critique before formal feedback. A post-review response is a disciplined repair-and-response process tied to actual comments.

## P023 pre-submission red flags

Use P023 as a tough reviewer lens. Flag the issue, explain why it matters, then propose a manuscript repair.

| Red flag | Reviewer concern | Audit question | Repair move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Summary-list literature review | The review is a bibliography with commentary, not synthesis. | Does the review show patterns, themes, contradictions, and gaps across evidence? | Rebuild around themes/debates/gaps; hand off to `references/cluster-literature-review.md`. |
| Weak synthesis | Studies are adjacent but not connected. | Are claims made across studies, or only one paper at a time? | Add cross-study comparison, trend, contrast, and gap sentences. |
| Overgeneralized conclusion | Claims exceed sample, setting, duration, design, or data. | Would the same claim be valid if a reviewer names the exact sample/design? | Reduce claim scope; hand off to `references/discussion-conclusion.md`. |
| Superficial limitations | Limitations are token labels without interpretive consequence. | Does each limitation explain how interpretation is affected? | Add consequence and scope; avoid self-destruction or generic apology. |
| Outdated references | Evidence base looks stale unless older works are seminal or the field legitimately relies on older anchor works. | Are current, relevant studies missing for this field, or are old sources dominating without justification? | Update sources when current evidence is required, identify seminal vs current, and repair literature positioning. |
| Generic/citation-mismatched AI content | Tone shifts, vague vocabulary, or citations do not support claims. | Does every claim have a matching source and does the prose sound field-specific? | Replace generic prose with claim-evidence alignment and verified citations. |
| Claim-evidence mismatch | The manuscript asserts more than it shows. | Can each major claim be traced to data, method, literature, or cited evidence? | Build a claim-evidence-scope table and cut unsupported claims. |

## Claim-scope audit

Before submission or resubmission, test high-risk claims:

| Claim | Evidence source | Design/sample boundary | Allowed scope | Repair if overclaiming |
|---|---|---|---|---|
|  | data / literature / theory / method |  |  | narrow / qualify / cite / move / delete |

Rules:

- Do not let the conclusion claim a field-wide recommendation from a small, local, short-duration, exploratory, or single-setting study.
- Do not turn an association into a causal claim unless the design supports causality.
- Do not treat lack of prior exact topic match as sufficient novelty.
- Do not hide weak evidence behind polished academic language.

## P025 major-revision stance

Treat major revisions as a repair path, not a rejection. The response should be serious, specific, and non-defensive.

Core principles:

- Address every reviewer/editor point, including small requests and implied concerns.
- Identify the underlying issue behind the comment: logic gap, missing literature, overreaching Discussion, unclear Methods, weak problem statement, misaligned Results, unsupported conclusion, or presentation issue.
- Repair the manuscript first; then craft the response.
- Specify what changed and where: section, paragraph, page/line, table/figure, appendix, or supplement if known. If locations are not known, use explicit placeholders and do not invent them.
- When agreeing, state the change and its purpose.
- When disagreeing, do so rarely and with evidence, field standards, data constraints, or journal instructions. Avoid defensiveness.
- Avoid vague response language such as “addressed,” “revised accordingly,” or “clarified” without describing the repair.

## Response-plan workflow

For each reviewer comment, move through five steps:

1. **Reviewer comment.** Quote or paraphrase the point briefly; preserve subparts.
2. **Underlying issue.** Diagnose what the reviewer is really asking for. Do they need evidence, clarity, scope reduction, method transparency, literature context, stronger logic, or formatting compliance?
3. **Manuscript repair.** Decide what must change in the manuscript, not just in the response letter. Repairs can include added synthesis, narrowed claim, expanded methods detail, reorganized Discussion, new limitation, revised figure/table note, updated citation, or deleted overclaim.
4. **Response text strategy.** Choose stance: agree and revise; partially agree and revise with boundary; respectfully disagree with evidence; explain no change because request conflicts with data/design/journal rule.
5. **Evidence/location check.** Ensure the response names where the change appears and that any rebuttal has support.

## Limited response-letter scaffold

Use this scaffold only after reviewer comments and manuscript repair decisions are visible. Keep placeholders where locations, line numbers, data, citations, or exact changes are unknown.

1. **Opening note:** thank the editor/reviewer briefly and state that the manuscript has been revised in response to the comments.
2. **Point-by-point format:** preserve each reviewer comment or subcomment.
3. **Response logic:** state whether the authors agree, partially agree, or respectfully disagree with evidence.
4. **Manuscript repair:** name the actual change: added synthesis, narrowed claim, clarified method, reorganized Discussion, added limitation, updated citation, revised table/figure text, or deleted overclaim.
5. **Location:** provide section/page/line/table/figure if known; otherwise use `[section/page/line to confirm]`.
6. **Evidence boundary:** if no change was made, explain why with data, design, field standard, or journal guidance; do not rely on defensiveness.

## Common reviewer-comment translations

| Reviewer wording | Likely underlying issue | Repair direction | Handoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| “Argument is unclear” | Aim, gap, Results, and claims do not form a traceable line. | Build aim-method-result-discussion map; clarify contribution. | `references/manuscript-and-results.md`, `references/cluster-gap-problem.md` |
| “Literature is incomplete” | Missing key voices, current evidence, or synthesis. | Add targeted literature and cross-study synthesis. | `references/cluster-literature-review.md` |
| “Discussion overstates findings” | Interpretation outruns design/data. | Narrow claims, add limitations, tie implications to findings. | `references/discussion-conclusion.md` |
| “Methods are unclear” | Reader cannot follow what was done or why. | Add method detail/justification; align analyses with aims. | `references/manuscript-and-results.md` |
| “Conclusion too broad” | Closing claim exceeds evidence. | Compress and scope contribution/recommendation. | `references/discussion-conclusion.md` |
| “Problem not compelling” | Population/setting/gap/stakes are unclear. | Repair opening problem/gap logic. | `references/cluster-gap-problem.md` |

## Red-flag audit workflow

When auditing a draft before submission:

1. Identify document genre, stage, target audience/journal if supplied, and desired harshness level. If missing, proceed as a general reviewer-facing audit.
2. Scan the opening for problem/gap/stakes clarity.
3. Check literature review synthesis: themes, patterns, contradictions, gaps, source currency, citation-claim match.
4. Check manuscript alignment: aim -> Methods -> Results -> Discussion -> Conclusion.
5. Check claim scope: data strength, design, sample, setting, causality, generalizability.
6. Check limitations: specificity, consequence, and relation to interpretation.
7. Check AI/prose risk: generic language, unsupported claims, citation mismatch, tone shifts.
8. Produce a prioritized repair plan: high-risk conceptual issues first, then section organization, then prose.

## Handoffs

- `references/manuscript-and-results.md`: whole-manuscript alignment, Results discipline, Methods-Results traceability, publication-readiness repair.
- `references/cluster-literature-review.md`: summary-list review, missing literature, weak synthesis, outdated evidence, citation-claim mismatch.
- `references/cluster-gap-problem.md`: weak problem statement, vague gap, unconvincing novelty, unclear aims/objectives.
- `references/discussion-conclusion.md`: overreaching Discussion, superficial limitations, vague implications, broad Conclusion.
- `references/language-and-outreach.md`: tone, hedging, response phrasing, and concise reviewer-facing language after the repair logic is clear.
