# Front-End, Concept, and Proposal Packaging

## Load when

Load this file when the user is preparing or auditing a concept paper, formal proposal, grant-like or specific-aims-like front end, qual/pre-qual proposal, thesis Chapter 1, dissertation introduction, supervisor-facing research memo, committee memo, or early research-idea package. Load `references/routing-map.md` first if the user has not specified the genre or if the task mixes proposal, manuscript, literature review, methods demands, or AI-assisted source-work defensibility.

Use this file to package validated research logic. If the gap, problem, or RQs are weak, hand off first to `references/cluster-gap-problem.md`; do not hide weak reasoning inside a polished proposal structure.

## Content-level transfer doctrine

P026 transfers by content and audit logic, not by rigid format. Its front-end checks can support proposals, specific aims, qual proposals, supervisor memos, grant-like pages, and manuscript Introductions, but it is not a universal Chapter 1 template. Preserve the genre distinction:

- A **thesis Chapter 1** may need explicit background, problem statement, purpose, RQs/objectives, significance, definitions, scope, delimitations, assumptions, and document-specific conventions.
- A **proposal** must persuade a supervisor, committee, funder, or reviewer that the project is important, grounded, feasible, and methodologically coherent.
- A **specific-aims-like page** is compressed and argumentative: problem, gap, central objective, aims, expected outcomes, significance, and feasibility must be visible quickly.
- A **supervisor memo** should be shorter, decision-oriented, and honest about uncertainties, next readings, design choices, and advisor questions.
- A **manuscript Introduction** borrows the narrowing logic but should follow manuscript conventions and hand off to `references/manuscript-and-results.md` when whole-paper alignment is needed.

## Genre guidance

| Genre | Best use | Core shape | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Concept paper | Early packaging of an idea for feedback or prewriting. | Problem, background/rationale, RQ/methodology, expected findings or conceptual contribution, final significance. | Overformalizing before the idea is coherent. |
| Formal proposal | Planning a study for committee, supervisor, institution, or funder-like review. | Title/front matter as needed, introduction, literature review, research design, implications, references, timeline/budget if required. | Treating a checklist as proof of logic. |
| Thesis Chapter 1 | Front-end setup for a dissertation or thesis. | Focused background, problem, purpose/objectives/RQs, significance, definitions, scope, delimitations, assumptions. | Using broad background or detailed Chapter 2 synthesis too early. |
| Specific-aims-like page | Compressed case for a study or project. | Problem/stakes, gap, objective, aims, expected outcome, significance, design fit. | Overclaiming grant conventions beyond supplied requirements. |
| Supervisor memo | Decision aid for discussion and iteration. | Working title, idea in one paragraph, gap/problem evidence, possible RQs, method options, risks, next decisions. | Pretending uncertainty is settled. |

## Packaging workflow

1. **Identify genre and audience.** Name who will read it and what decision they must make: approve topic, guide design, fund/endorse, admit to qual stage, or request revisions.
2. **Import validated gap/problem logic.** From `references/cluster-gap-problem.md`, bring forward the candidate gap type, evidence status, population/setting, stakes, and researchable focus.
3. **Compress literature to front-end function.** Use `references/cluster-literature-review.md` only to support the rationale. Do not build full Chapter 2 synthesis inside a proposal opening unless requested by the genre.
4. **Write the focused background.** Narrow from field context to the specific study space. Each paragraph should move toward the problem, not merely display knowledge.
5. **State the problem.** Show what is happening, to whom/where, why it matters now, what the literature or practice response has not addressed, and what consequence follows.
6. **Align purpose, RQs/objectives, and aims.** Each objective should answer the problem, be specific, realistic, and testable or investigable through the proposed method.
7. **Check significance.** Specify who benefits and how the study contributes to theory, practice, policy, method, or future research. Avoid "adds to the literature" as the only significance.
8. **Define boundaries.** Add operational definitions for key terms; clarify scope, delimitations, assumptions, and what the study will not cover.
9. **Fit method/design at front-end level.** State why the design can answer the RQs and address the gap. Keep details at alignment level unless the user supplies protocol requirements. For high-level non-meta design-family fit, hand off to `references/method-fit-triage.md`; for meta-analysis or evidence synthesis, hand off to `references/method-meta-analysis.md`.
10. **Check AI/source-work quality if relevant.** If AI helped generate, summarize, outline, polish, or cite proposal material, verify source visibility, authorial ownership, meaning preservation, citation support, and target-rule/disclosure status.
11. **Audit flow.** The front end should read as a chain: focused background -> problem -> gap/stakes -> purpose -> RQs/objectives/aims -> significance -> feasible design.

## Front-end alignment checks

| Check | Diagnostic question | Repair action |
|---|---|---|
| Focused background | Does every paragraph narrow toward the specific problem? | Remove broad textbook setup; add context-specific bridge sentences. |
| Problem statement | Are population/setting, specific issue, gap, evidence, and stakes visible? | Rebuild via `references/cluster-gap-problem.md`. |
| Objectives/RQs | Do they directly address the problem and stay measurable or investigable? | Convert vague intentions into aligned questions or aims. |
| Significance | Is the contribution more than "more literature"? | Name theory, practice, policy, method, population, or decision benefits. |
| Definitions | Are key concepts operationalized enough for readers to interpret the study? | Add concise definitions and specify usage boundaries. |
| Scope/delimitations | Does the reader know what is inside and outside the study? | State population, setting, time, variables/phenomenon, and exclusions. |
| Assumptions | Are necessary methodological or contextual assumptions visible? | List only assumptions that affect interpretation or feasibility. |
| Design fit | Can the method plausibly answer the RQs and address the gap type? | Revise RQs, design rationale, or gap framing. |
| Flow | Do sections feel like one argument rather than disconnected blocks? | Add transitions and reorder around the problem-to-aim chain. |

## Boundary gates

- Do not treat proposal, thesis, manuscript Introduction, supervisor memo, and outreach as interchangeable.
- Do not use P026 as a rigid format template outside thesis contexts.
- Do not provide detailed methods protocols beyond front-end fit unless the user supplies standards. Use `references/method-fit-triage.md` for high-level non-meta design-family alignment and `references/method-meta-analysis.md` for high-level meta-analysis/evidence-synthesis planning.
- Do not overstate grant-specific conventions; use specific-aims-like logic only as a compact argumentative front-end pattern unless funder/program guidance is supplied or retrieved.
- Do not build a full literature review here. Use selective literature to justify the problem; hand off detailed synthesis to `references/cluster-literature-review.md`.
- Do not polish a proposal whose gap, problem, or RQs are unsupported.
- Do not let AI-generated rationale, citations, claims, or aims substitute for visible source evidence and human-owned research logic.

## Handoffs

- Use `references/cluster-gap-problem.md` for novelty, gap taxonomy, problem-statement repair, RQ/aim alignment, hypothesis logic, or weak absence-only claims.
- Use `references/cluster-literature-review.md` for detailed literature synthesis, theme maps, related-work architecture, or source-grounded AI-assisted paper comparison.
- Use `references/manuscript-and-results.md` when converting the front end into a manuscript Introduction or aligning aims, Methods, Results, and contribution across a full paper.
- Use `references/method-fit-triage.md` when a proposal/front-end question asks whether the RQ, objective, aim, hypothesis, evidence type, and broad design family fit each other.
- Use `references/method-meta-analysis.md` when the proposed design is a meta-analysis or quantitative evidence synthesis and the user needs a high-level workflow check.
- Use `references/language-and-outreach.md` for final academic phrasing, significance wording, concise supervisor outreach, or person-to-person research communication after the logic is coherent.
- Use `references/ai-assisted-source-work-quality.md` when AI assistance affected source summaries, citations, proposal prose, aims, disclosure/compliance awareness, or submission/committee defensibility.
